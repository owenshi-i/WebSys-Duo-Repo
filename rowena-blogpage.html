March 3, 2026 - NOTE TO READERS

My First Blog Entry

This is my first official blog post for our project. I want to share the journey of how I started this portfolio and blog, and the challenges I faced along the way.

I found my duo partner on February 23. I had already started some preparations before I found her. I had created our repository, homepage, and student profiles for both of us, and I even shared the repo link with her. However, I wasn’t able to work on it immediately because I was dealing with an eye infection that lasted about a week. I had planned to start on Monday, but I had to return to the hospital for a follow-up check, which delayed my work further. Today is the first day I was able to fully manage my time and work on this blog. It was challenging at first, especially since this was my first time doing a project like this. I faced difficulties such as understanding site examples, organizing files, and figuring out the structure, but I gradually overcame them by researching, experimenting, and asking questions when needed.

Since I wasn’t able to post my daily blog entries on the days I missed, I plan to cover those days by recalling what I did last week. I am determined to work hard and finish all the remaining blog entries before our project presentation. I am even willing to dedicate extra time to make up for the days I missed and ensure that my portfolio is complete and polished.

February 23, 2026 — Day 1

Starting the Project

Today I officially started working on our Web Systems and Design project that was assigned to us last Thursday, February 19. I didn’t have a duo partner for the past few days, so I wasn’t able to start immediately. Today I’ve found my partner, Elisha Faith Alcazar, I began creating our repository, homepage, and profile pages. I also shared the repository link with her so we could collaborate.

Since this was my first time working on a project like this, I spent some time learning how to set up the GitHub repository, create branches, and understand commits. I practiced making my first commits and pushing changes to the repository. To learn more and understand the process better, I also watched lots of tutorial videos. At first, it felt overwhelming and confusing, especially figuring out how to organize files and structure the project properly. But I gradually got the hang of it by experimenting, researching, and asking questions when needed.

At the same time, I was dealing with an eye infection, my eyes were swollen and painful, but I tried to work on the project anyway since I needed to cooperate with my partner. I was able to manage my condition just enough to start, and I instructed her on what tasks to do while we organized the work together.

Because of my condition, I also excused myself from class today, including both our Networking and Web Systems and Design sessions. In Web Systems, we had a quiz scheduled, but I wasn’t able to take it. I informed ma’am about my situation and asked when I could take the quiz so I can stay on track with my grades while continuing to work on the project.

Working on this project teaches us how important preparation, patience, and persistence are. It shows that even when facing challenges like being sick, we can still make progress if we focus on what we can do. Watching tutorials and practicing hands-on helps us understand new skills better, and collaborating with a partner requires clear communication and guidance. This experience shows that learning and perseverance go hand in hand when completing a project successfully.

I needed to focus on recovery, and I’m glad I could take the time to rest without falling behind in class.

February 25, 2026 — Day 3

A Day of Rest and Reality

In observance of the EDSA People Power Revolution anniversary, our classes were suspended and shifted to asynchronous mode until Friday. I was really thankful for this because it gave me time to rest and finally focus on my check-up. At this point, my eye condition had already worsened, I honestly had no idea what happened in my classes because I wasn’t able to attend.

We decided to have my eyes checked, and we spent almost the whole afternoon at the clinic because of the long lines. After the check-up, the doctor gave me a prescription for several medications. The total cost almost reached more than ₱2,000, which was really heavy for us financially.

We didn’t have enough money to buy everything at once, so we decided to purchase the medicines little by little. At that time, we didn’t bring enough cash, so I was only able to take my medicine around 8 PM when my father arrived from work and bought it for me.

I was also thankful because my uncle had leftover medicine that was the same as what was prescribed to me. He previously had an eye infection too and went to the same clinic. Because of that, we were able to save more than ₱700, and I only needed to buy the remaining medicines gradually.

This experience taught me how important it is to be grateful even during difficult times, to appreciate the help of family, and to understand that health should always come first. It also reminded me that rest is sometimes necessary, not a weakness.

February 26, 2026 — Day 4

Art, Assignments, and Recovery

Today was the submission of our PathFit 4 group painting activity. Our theme was “Kulay ng Pag-asa: Buhay Estudyante sa Bicol University.” We had already finished our painting last week, and we worked together as a group to create an artwork that represents the life, struggles, and hopes of students in Bicol University.

Aside from the artwork itself, we were also required to explain its meaning and create a video presentation. Unfortunately, I was not able to participate in the video because of my eye condition. I was also unable to properly update our instructor about my situation, and there were no clear updates in our group chat, which made me worried about what might happen. Because of my condition, I wasn’t able to assist or manage our group the way I usually would.

Since classes are still asynchronous, we were also given a task in Understanding the Self to watch the movie 3 Idiots and write a review. I haven’t watched it yet, but I plan to do so once my condition improves. Yesterday, my eye condition was already bad, and today it became even worse. This is only my second time taking the prescribed medicine, so I understand that recovery will not be instant.

This experience taught me the importance of patience, understanding my limits, and communicating properly during group work. It also reminded me that health should always come first, even when responsibilities continue to pile up.

February 27, 2026 — Day 5

Rest, Recovery, and Resilience

It’s Friday and classes are still conducted online. For our Science, Technology, and Society subject, there was no update on whether we had an online class or not. The same thing happened in our Information Management subject, there were no announcements or instructions given.

Sir Ryan, as the IT department head, only reminded us that attendance would still be important in the coming days. Because of the lack of updates, I decided to focus on my recovery and rest for the day.

However, instead of improving, my condition became worse. I developed two painful bumps on my eye, which I think might be styes. This may have been caused by constantly removing my eyelashes because my eye kept producing discharge and mucus.

This was one of the hardest moments for me. My eye infection started when my eyelash scratched the white part of my eye, which later became infected. Since there was mascara on my eyelashes and the infection hadn’t healed yet, my condition worsened and led to new complications.

Even though it was painful and frustrating, I reminded myself to stay strong. This experience tested my patience, but I know that I can get through this with proper care, rest, and determination.

This experience taught me that health should always be a priority. No matter how important school tasks and deadlines are, they become meaningless if my body cannot keep up. I learned that it is okay to slow down, rest, and ask for understanding when needed. Taking care of myself is not a sign of weakness but a necessary step to recover and continue moving forward with strength and determination.

February 28, 2026 — Day 6

Between Recovery and Missed Opportunities

Today is Saturday. We were supposed to have our Ethics class at the Bicol University College of Social Sciences and Philosophy, but it was cancelled because of a cybersecurity webinar event happening on campus. Although the event focused on technology and security awareness, I wasn’t able to attend due to my condition.

Despite that, I felt thankful because the cancelled class gave me another chance to rest and focus on my recovery. At the same time, I couldn’t help but feel a bit sad and regretful. As a CIRCUITS officer, I wanted to be there to help and participate in the event, but my health did not allow me to do so.

As for my eye condition, it stayed the same. The two bumps are still there and continue to produce discharge, making it hard for me to open my eyes every morning. Thankfully, my mom always helps me by preparing warm water so I can gently soak my face, which helps soften the discharge and makes it easier for me to open my eyes.

This afternoon, my mom also decided to try a traditional way of checking my condition. It was my first time experiencing it, and honestly, I don’t really believe in these things. I even shouted in surprise when she placed the black charcoal on my skin. Still, I’m willing to try anything that might help me recover faster.

This day taught me that healing requires patience and openness. Even when I miss opportunities and responsibilities, I am reminded that my health comes first. Sometimes recovery means accepting help, trying different approaches, and trusting that better days will come once I am well again.

March 1, 2026 — Day 7

A Quiet Day of Rest and Prayer

Today is Sunday. As usual, there were no classes, so it was supposed to be a rest day. Nothing much happened throughout the day, but I was still suffering from my eye condition.

Last night was especially difficult. The pain was intense, and I could barely sleep. I remember just lying in bed and praying, asking for strength and hoping that the pain would finally ease. I kept telling myself that I hoped this would be the last day I would feel that level of discomfort.

As of today, my condition is mostly the same. My eye still produces discharge, so I need to clean it frequently. However, compared to the past days, it is no longer as swollen as before. The two bumps or styes are still present, but overall, my eye feels a bit more manageable now.

This day reminded me that healing doesn’t always happen instantly. Sometimes progress is slow, but even small improvements matter. Through rest, prayer, and patience, I learned to appreciate every little sign that things are getting better.
